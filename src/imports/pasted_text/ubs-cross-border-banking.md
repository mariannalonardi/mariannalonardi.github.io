# Supporting your banking life across borders

![Mockup-Orizzontal-02.jpg](attachment:58021a1b-358b-4070-bc90-fd11d28d19e3:Mockup-Orizzontal-02.jpg)

## Abstract

The project proposes a simplified and more intuitive version of the UBS banking app, specifically designed for Italy–Switzerland cross-border workers. Its goal is to build trust through clarity, transparency, and the reduction of friction points typically found in multi-currency and multi-bank management. It integrates features such as instant monitoring of salaries and exchange rates, cost visibility, smart automations, and financial education tools. The core metaphor is that of a unified ecosystem: a clear and coherent space that replaces the complexity of two banking systems, enabling easier, more informed, and smoother financial management.

## Research and Development

The project emerges from extensive research involving desk analysis, surveys, and in-person interviews with cross-border workers. The context of use is shaped by the unique financial ecosystem between Italy and Switzerland: two currencies, two regulatory systems, and dual banking infrastructures. The development focuses on addressing identified pain points: lack of transparency, fragmented monitoring, complex multi-currency interactions, and slow everyday operations. The project stands as a scalable solution that can be extended to other cross-border contexts or multi-currency user groups.

## Interface and Interaction Modality

The project is a digital artefact: a mobile app. Interaction occurs primarily through touch and clear visual interfaces, supported by smart notifications and occasional natural-language guidance. The senses involved are sight and touch. The app integrates with Swiss and Italian banking systems, supports CHF and EUR, and runs on smartphones without the need for additional physical devices. It is not entirely new but an evolution of an existing banking app, enriched with functionalities tailored to the specific needs of cross-border workers.

## User Experience

From a functional perspective, the user experience is:
• **Simple and intuitive**: offering quick understanding of salaries, exchange
rates, total costs, and money movements.
• **Pleasurable and smooth**: reducing operational friction and streamlining daily financial tasks.
• **Reliable**: providing precise notifications that give users full control
over their finances.
• **Customizable and adaptable**: with automations,configurable multi-currency views, and personalised financial education.
• **Inclusive**: designed for people navigating between two countries, two
languages, and two banking systems.
• **Integrable**: capable of connecting with external banking services and fitting naturally into daily activities such as payments, international transfers, and expense
management.

# The Challenge

**Objective:** Build trust with cross-border workers through a simplified, intuitive UBS app experience.

**Problem:** Daily banking tasks are complicated due to dual accounts, currencies (CHF/EUR), and differing regulations. Users face friction in total salary checks, currency conversion, notifications, and transfers.

### The Brief

The goal of this project is to redesign the UBS mobile app to better support Italian–Swiss cross-border workers in their everyday financial life.
For this target, banking is not just about managing money, but about navigating two countries, two currencies, and two regulatory systems at the same time.

Our brief was to create a clear, simple, and intuitive experience that reduces friction in daily banking while building trust. The app should support essential tasks such as salary checks, currency exchange, payments, and transfers, while also integrating financial education in a natural and non-intrusive way.
The experience should feel smooth, reliable, and tailored to the real needs of people living and working across borders.

### The context

Cross-border workers face a level of financial complexity that traditional banking apps are not designed for. Managing two bank accounts, converting between CHF and EUR, and understanding fees and exchange rates are part of their daily routine, not occasional tasks.

While the current UBS app already covers core banking needs, it does not fully address the specific challenges of cross-border life. Users experience fragmented monitoring, limited visibility on cross-border tools, complex multi-currency overviews, and low clarity on total costs.
As a result, even simple actions become time-consuming and stressful, reducing confidence and trust in the banking experience.

# Experience Mapping

The experience mapping phase focused on understanding the real, everyday journey of Italian–Swiss cross-border workers when managing their finances.
We mapped the main touchpoints of their banking experience, from checking their salary to exchanging currencies, paying bills, and monitoring expenses across two countries.

This process highlighted how even simple actions become fragmented and cognitively demanding due to the need to switch between currencies, accounts, and banking systems. The experience map helped us visualize moments of friction, uncertainty, and loss of control, revealing clear opportunities to simplify flows, reduce steps, and improve clarity throughout the journey.

Multiple experience mappings were created depending on the personas analyzed.

### Benchmark

A comparative analysis was conducted on existing banking and financial apps that offer multi-currency or cross-border functionality.

While these solutions generally provide basic tools for managing accounts and foreign exchange, they are often designed for general users rather than specifically addressing the needs of cross-border workers.

Most of the apps analyzed do not offer clear visibility into total foreign exchange costs, provide limited contextual guidance, and do not significantly integrate training or automation. This confirmed the opportunity for UBS to differentiate itself by offering a more personalized, transparent, and supportive cross-border experience.

The analysis focused on both traditional and digital banks, focusing on the latter and specifically on Yuh because it is the only digital bank with a real Swiss IBAN.

### User Research

User research was a fundamental part of the project and was carried out through desk research, surveys, and in-person interviews.
The goal was to understand who cross-border workers are, how they currently manage their finances, and which challenges they face on a daily basis.

Research findings showed that managing multiple currencies is a constant activity rather than an occasional task. Users frequently need to check salary payments, monitor exchange rates, and understand fees, but often struggle with fragmented information and unclear costs. These insights strongly informed the definition of the problem and the design of the solution.

![image.png](attachment:727c5cbf-3ebf-4992-9ea2-ab2a918b5a9f:image.png)

### Key Findings

The research phase led to several key findings:

- Checking salaries, exchange rates, and fees is not straightforward and lacks transparency.
- Everyday banking operations involve too many steps and require excessive time.
- Notifications and monitoring tools are insufficient, causing users to lose control of their finances.
- Managing two banks and two currencies significantly increases complexity and cognitive load.

These findings highlighted the need for a clearer, more unified, and more intuitive banking experience for cross-border workers.

# Problem definition

Cross-border workers need to manage their finances across two countries, two currencies, and two regulatory systems, but current banking solutions do not adequately support this complexity.
The lack of transparency around exchange rates and fees, combined with fragmented monitoring and manual processes, leads to frustration, uncertainty, and reduced trust.

The core problem is therefore how to simplify financial management for cross-border workers while increasing clarity, control, and confidence in their daily banking activities.

# Solution

The proposed solution introduces a dedicated experience within the UBS app designed specifically for cross-border workers.

The goal is to support users in understanding, monitoring, and optimizing currency exchanges between CHF and EUR through clarity, automation, and education.

The solution combines three main pillars:

- **Transparency**, by clearly visualizing exchange rates and related fees;
- **Decision support**, through an integrated account that shows both currencies, alerts, historical data, and automated exchange options;
- **Empowerment**, via a learning and reward system that builds financial awareness over time.

By centralizing these features inside the UBS ecosystem, the solution prevents users from relying on external fintech apps and strengthens trust in the bank as a long-term financial partner.

# Target

The primary target group is **cross-border workers commuting between Switzerland and Italy**, who receive their salary in CHF and regularly transfer money to an account in EUR.

These users are generally:

- Highly sensitive to exchange rates and hidden fees
- Repetitive users of currency exchange services
- Financially cautious but not necessarily expert investors

Although the design and testing focused on the Swiss–Italian corridor for geographical and contextual reasons, the solution is **fully scalable** and can be easily adapted to other cross-border contexts such as **Switzerland–France** or **Switzerland–Germany**, by changing currencies, regulations, and localized content.

# User Flows

The user flow starts from the moment the user opens the UBS app and is automatically recognized as a cross-border worker.

From the dedicated home view, users can:

- Monitor exchange rates, balances, and historical trends
- Set personalized minimum and maximum exchange targets
- Choose between manual or automatic currency conversion
- Receive notifications when optimal exchange conditions are met

The flow also integrates alternative paths for learning and investing, allowing users to:

- Engage with educational content and gamified challenges
- Track rewards and progress
- Access simple and advanced investment plans in CHF

The flow is designed to be flexible, allowing users to remain in control while progressively introducing smarter and more automated behaviors.

# User testing, heuristics & lean validation

The solution was tested with **real users fitting the target profile**, including cross-border workers commuting between Switzerland and Italy.

Testing activities included task-based usability testing and observation of user behavior.

Key usability heuristics such as **clarity of system status**, **user control**, and **error prevention** were evaluated throughout the sessions.

Lean validation principles were applied by:

- Testing early concepts through low and mid fidelity prototypes
- Iterating based on direct user feedback
- Prioritizing features that demonstrated clear user value

Overall, users responded positively, especially to transparency features and automated exchange options.

# Conclusions, reflections and next steps

This project demonstrates how focusing on a specific, often overlooked user group can reveal meaningful opportunities for innovation within an established banking ecosystem.

Cross-border workers do not necessarily require more features, but rather **more clarity, predictability, and confidence** when managing their finances across different countries. The research and testing phases showed that the lack of transparency around exchange rates and fees is not just a usability issue, but a **trust issue**—one that directly influences users’ long-term behavior and platform loyalty.

Through this project, we learned that:

- Transparency and control are key drivers in financial decision-making
- Automation can significantly reduce cognitive load and financial anxiety
- Educational elements, when designed with restraint, can empower users without overwhelming them

From a design perspective, working within an existing brand such as UBS highlighted the importance of balancing innovation with consistency. Introducing new interactions and services while respecting established visual and interaction guidelines proved essential to maintaining credibility and user trust.

Looking ahead, the solution could evolve by an integration with broader investment and savings strategies.
