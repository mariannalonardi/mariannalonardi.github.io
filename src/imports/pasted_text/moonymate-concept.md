## Abstract

Moonymate is an interactive 15×15 cm cube designed to help children aged 3 to 7 remember and complete their bedtime routine in a playful and reassuring way. Through physical interaction, lights, sound, and movement, the cube guides children step by step through four simple pre-sleep activities, encouraging autonomy. Once the routine is completed, Moonymate rewards the child with a rising moon, a bedtime story, and a soft night light, transforming bedtime into a calm and meaningful ritual.

## Concept

Bedtime can be a challenging moment for both children and parents. Repeating the same instructions every evening often leads to stress, distraction, and resistance. Moonymate was designed to support children in building independence while turning the bedtime routine into a playful, reassuring, and structured experience.

Moonymate is a tangible interactive object that combines physical manipulation, visual feedback, sound, and storytelling. The cube must be rotated step by step, starting from the first face and ending with the last one. Each face represents a specific bedtime action:

1. Preparing the school backpack
2. Putting on pajamas
3. Brushing teeth
4. Turning off the light

After completing each action, the child presses a button on the side of the cube. This interaction activates the LEDs, confirms that the task has been successfully completed, and invites the child to rotate the cube to the next face. The sequential interaction helps children understand order, time, and progression, while reducing the cognitive load associated with remembering the routine.

Once the final action is completed, Moonymate rewards the child with a magical moment: a moon rises from the top of the cube through a servo mechanism. By pressing a central button on the top surface, a bedtime story starts playing through the integrated speakers.

After the story ends, the cube remains softly illuminated, functioning as a night light that provides comfort and a sense of security while the child falls asleep. In addition, Moonymate is connected via Wi-Fi to a mobile application that allows parents to remotely turn the cube on or off, maintaining parental control while leaving space for the child’s autonomy.

The combination of routine, reward, and multisensory interaction transforms Moonymate into a bedtime companion rather than a simple reminder device.

## User journey

The bedtime experience with Moonymate begins when the cube is activated by a parent through the connected app. The child is then invited to interact with the cube starting from the first face.

On each side, the child is encouraged to complete a specific action related to the bedtime routine. Once the action is done, the child presses one of the physical buttons located on the side of the cube. This input triggers a visual feedback through the LEDs, confirming success.

The cube is then rotated to the next face, guiding the child through the sequence in a clear and intuitive way. This process continues until all four actions are completed.

At the end of the routine, a small moon rises from the top of the cube using a servo mechanism. The child can press the button located at the center of the top surface to start a bedtime story played through the integrated speakers.

After the story finishes, the cube stays softly illuminated, creating a comforting night-light effect that helps the child feel safe and relaxed while falling asleep.

## Prototyping phase

The development of Moonymate took place over five days and followed a rapid, iterative design process.

**Day 1 – Problem Definition and Target Selection**

The first day was dedicated to defining the problem space and identifying the target audience. We focused on children aged 3 to 7 and the difficulties surrounding bedtime routines, especially the need for structure, repetition, and reassurance.

**Day 2 – Concept Sketching and Interaction Design**

On the second day, we created several hand-drawn sketches to explore the form of the cube, the rotation mechanism, and the placement of buttons, LEDs, and interactive elements. This phase was crucial to understanding how children would physically interact with the object and how the sequence of actions could be clearly communicated.

**Days 3–5 – Digital Modeling, Fabrication, and Electronics**

During the following days, we moved into digital fabrication. We designed both the external cube and the internal electronics box using Fusion 360. The internal structure of the cube, which contains the electronic components, is made of wood to ensure stability and protection. The external shell is made of plexiglass, allowing the light from the LEDs to be visible and enhancing the magical and child-friendly aesthetic of the object.

In parallel, we focused on the electronic components and their integration. Moonymate is powered by an Arduino and includes:

- A 16-LED strip running around the cube
- A 24-LED circular ring on the top surface
- Five physical buttons
- A 90-degree mini servo motor for the moon mechanism
- Speakers and a DFPlayer module for audio playback

The moon element was 3D printed, while decorative stickers were produced using a vinyl cutter to make the cube more visually appealing and child-friendly.

Assembling the object required careful coordination between structure, electronics, and aesthetics. Multiple iterations were necessary to ensure that all components fit correctly and that interactions were robust and intuitive for young users.
